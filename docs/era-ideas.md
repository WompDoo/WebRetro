# Era ideas

Each era is a snapshot year standing for a span of web history. Built so far: 1997 and 2005.
Everything on the page is site dressing: fake content and simple loops, never a working app.

| Era | Covers | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1997 Personal homepage | mid-90s to the 2000 crash | built |
| 2005 Web 2.0 | 2001–2009 | built |
| 2011 Social & mobile | 2010–2015 | ideas |
| 2018 Platform | 2016–2022 | ideas |
| 2024 AI | 2023–now | ideas |

Pattern every era follows: you arrive knowing the previous era's skills, and the page keeps the old look
until you learn the new ones. Each skill changes the page, the code you type, and what you can build.

## 2011 Social & mobile

- **The page doesn't fit on phones** until you learn responsive design. Before that, the site
  scrolls sideways inside a phone frame. Media queries are the "Learn CSS" moment of this era.
- **"Sign in with…" buttons** (parody company names, never real brands).
- **Stack Overflow copy-paste** as the typing tool: keystrokes paste whole answers, sometimes the wrong one.
- Like buttons, share counts, infinite scroll, app-install banners ("Get our app!").
- jQuery everything: `$(document).ready(...)` in the editor.
- **Masonry tiles**: this era's layout moment. You arrive with the glossy 2005 two-column blog;
  "Learn the masonry grid" breaks the page into Pinterest-style tiles of different sizes. The editor
  types `$('.grid').masonry({ itemSelector: '.tile' })`, because back then it was a jQuery plugin.
- **Parallax scrolling**: the site's background layers drift at different speeds as you scroll
  inside the browser window. Pure eye candy.
- **Scroll-jacking one-pagers**: a toggle with a trade-off, like the defenses. On: the site wins
  "Site of the Day" design awards (more traffic), but scrolling snaps section by section, some
  visitors leave, and the player has to live with it too while hunting viruses.

This is already a full curriculum (about 9 skills). New 2011 ideas should push something into 2018.

## 2018 Platform

- **Flat design**: learning it removes the 2005 gloss, one gradient at a time.
- **GDPR cookie walls** (May 2018): the 1997 pop-up comes back, now required by law.
  "Accept all" is big; "Reject all" hides behind "Manage options".
- **Full-screen timed ads** with a "Skip in 5" countdown. A new hazard: you can't close it until the
  timer ends, and it costs visitors while it's up. It never makes you fail.
- Ads everywhere: sticky footer ads, ads between every paragraph, "Please disable your ad blocker".
- Newsletter modals, push-notification prompts ("Allow notifications?"), chat bubbles.

## 2024 AI

- **"✨ Ask AI" chatbot bubble** on every page. Site dressing only: canned replies that are confidently
  wrong about your own site ("Your guestbook was founded in 1887 by a cat."). Like the guestbook entries,
  it's a rotating list of fake messages, not a real chatbot.
- AI-written filler content ("In today's fast-paced digital landscape…").
- **Stack Overflow fades out**: the typing tool becomes an AI pair programmer that writes most of
  the code, sometimes too much of it.
- AI scrapers as a hazard; CAPTCHAs asking you to click every traffic light.
- Ending: acquisition offers from parody tech giants, based on what you built.

## Pop-up rules (all eras)

- Every pop-up has a safe × in the title bar. Body buttons vary per pop-up: none, a safe close,
  only the scam button, a "click here" link, or both buttons being scams.
- Falling for a scam costs 10% of your money and posts what happened to the news.
